## The Specificity of Financial Language

In everyday English, the word *"liability"* might mean an annoyance or handicap. In accounting, it is an ordinary balance sheet line item. Similarly, the sentence *"Company announces a dividend reduction to fund R&D expansion"* contains negative words, but may signal long-term strategic growth.

General language models frequently misinterpret financial disclosures. **FinBERT** (a BERT architecture fine-tuned on corporate earnings calls and financial news) was specifically designed to classify financial statements into **Positive**, **Negative**, and **Neutral**.

### Sentiment Integration Workflow

1. Ingest real-time headlines via financial RSS feeds or news APIs.
2. Filter for company tickers (e.g. AAPL, NVDA, BTC).
3. Score each headline through the sentiment pipeline:
   - Positive score: $+1.0 \times \text{confidence}$
   - Negative score: $-1.0 \times \text{confidence}$
   - Neutral score: $0.0$
4. Compute an exponential moving average (EMA) of sentiment over a rolling 4-hour window.
5. In systematic trading, sentiment is best utilized as a **regime filter** rather than an outright trade trigger (e.g., restricting long positions when aggregate sentiment turns sharply negative).
